## Thread (7 posts)

**1/7**
Western Michigan isn't a throwaway Week 1 MAC opponent.

Defending MAC champs. Preseason favorites to repeat. And they're bringing back the guy who beat everyone for it.

Here's what the data actually says about Michigan's opener 🧵

**2/7**
Western's 2025 offense was one of the most run-heavy in the country — 65.1% of decisions were runs — built around QB Broc Lowry (963 rush yards, 14 rush TDs, MAC Offensive Player of the Year).

Passing was the weak spot: bottom-quarter nationally, adjusted for opponent.

**3/7**
The defense is what actually carried Western in 2025 — a top-35 opponent-adjusted unit, particularly against the pass.

So which half of the team shows up in 2026 matters a lot. That's where the roster continuity story gets interesting.

**4/7**
Good news for Western: Lowry's back, so is RB Jalen Buckley.

We built our own roster-continuity audit (not borrowed from anyone) and found Lowry + Buckley alone account for 82% of the offense's returning production.

**5/7**
The bad news: the defense that made Western a problem is largely rebuilt.

Snap-weighted research has Western returning just 20% of D-line snaps and 12% at linebacker — the single lowest continuity number on either roster in this matchup.

**6/7**
That's not the whole story, though. Western didn't hand the front seven to freshmen — it imported experienced transfers from Ohio State, Houston and North Carolina. And the secondary (55% snap continuity, top-25 adjusted pass defense) is mostly still there.

**7/7**
Michigan's clearest path through: force Western into obvious passing downs. The rush-vs-pass success gap is the widest split in their whole profile.
